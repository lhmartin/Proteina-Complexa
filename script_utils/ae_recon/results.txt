Frozen Proteina-Complexa autoencoder: side-chain + sequence reconstruction
given the true backbone.  Re-run 2026-11-20 after a normalisation fix.

CORRECTION (2026-11-20).  The earlier numbers in this file (Fv mean 0.042 A)
were a factor sqrt(3) too low.  ae_recon_test.py divided the squared-deviation
sum by 3 * n_atoms, which is the per-COORDINATE mean square, not the per-ATOM
one -- `valid` is [n, 37], so valid.sum() already counts atoms.  Fixed; every
figure below is from the corrected run.  The ratio (0.80x) is unchanged, since
the error was a constant factor.

WHAT THIS MEASURES.  This is a PARTIAL autoencoder.  decode(z, ca_coors_nm,
mask) receives the TRUE CA coordinates, so the backbone is given, not
reconstructed; the 8-dim per-residue latent carries the side chains and the
sequence.  These RMSDs are therefore side-chain-and-sequence fidelity
CONDITIONED ON A CORRECT BACKBONE.  That is the right quantity for our purpose
-- the flow model's local_latents training label is exactly
autoencoder.encode(batch)["z_latent"] -- but it is NOT "the AE reconstructs a
structure to within X A", and should not be quoted that way.  RMSD is the root
mean squared per-atom distance over all heavy atoms of each residue type
(decoder.get_atom_mask()[aatype]), no superposition: inputs and reconstruction
are already in the same frame.  The ~7.7-8.1 atoms/residue column confirms all
heavy atoms are included, not just the 4-5 backbone ones.

READING.  Antibody Fvs reconstruct slightly BETTER than the non-antibody
controls (0.80x), and sequence recovery is 100.0% on every input, so the frozen
AE's latent space is not an Ig-fold bottleneck and does not need retraining or
unfreezing for antibody work.  Larger assemblies degrade mildly (0.072 Fv ->
0.085-0.107 for 3-chain complexes), tracking residue count rather than
antibody-ness.  Median per-atom deviation stays at 0.044-0.063 A while RMSD is
higher, i.e. the error is a small tail of atoms, not a uniform blur -- consistent
with a few long or surface side chains being placed imperfectly.
